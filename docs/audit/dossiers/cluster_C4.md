# Cluster C4

Loan projection: amortization start not advanced by paymentsMade; paymentNumber from post-filter index; completion drops final payment / mutates state wrong

Source files implicated:
- app/lib/logic/projectionEngine/loanProjections.ts
- app/contexts/FinancialContext/actions/transactionActions.ts

## Member findings (from first-round analysts)

### [F7] (critical/logic-error) Loan amortization projection dates are shifted earlier by paymentsMade months (future payments wrong, tail payments never projected)
- File: app/lib/logic/projectionEngine/loanProjections.ts : 27-43
- Why wrong: The amortization schedule correctly uses the remaining balance and remaining term, but it always starts the schedule dates at the ORIGINAL loan startDate (parseDate(rule.startDate)). calculateAmortizationSchedule begins at startDate and adds one month per step. Because paymentsMade is only subtracted from the term length (not used to advance the start date), the remaining payments are placed at month 0, 1, 2 ... from the original start instead of starting at the next-due payment date. The result is that every projected loan payment date is shifted earlier by paymentsMade months, the already-paid early months are re-projected as future payments, and the final paymentsMade real payment dates are never produced at all. (The installment generator at installmentProjections.ts:35-37 does this correctly by advancing currentDate by installmentsPaid months first; the loan generator omits that step, confirming the asymmetry.) The paymentNumber labeling (paymentsMade + index + 1) also no longer matches the date, so paymentBreakdown.paymentNumber is attached to the wrong calendar date.
- Scenario: Loan: startDate 2025-01-15, termMonths 12, paymentsMade 3 (Jan/Feb/Mar already completed). With remainingPayments=9 and startDate=2025-01-15, the schedule produces dates 2025-01-15, 02-15, 03-15, ... 09-15 (9 steps). Code labels 2025-01-15 as paymentNumber 4. Correct behavior: the next 9 payments should fall on 2025-04-15 through 2025-12-15 (payment numbers 4..12). Wrong output: payments are projected on Jan-Sep (three of them in the past / overlapping months already paid), and Oct/Nov/Dec 2025 payments are missing entirely. The calendar, cash-flow forecast, and bill-coverage all show loan payments on the wrong dates, understating the loan's true future cash outflow window.
- Suggested fix: Advance the schedule start date by paymentsMade months before generating, e.g. startDate: addMonths(parseDate(rule.startDate), loanConfig.paymentsMade) (mirroring installmentProjections), or generate the full schedule from the original start and slice off the first paymentsMade steps so the remaining steps keep their original calendar dates.

### [F13] (high/correctness) Completing a loan payment drops the final payment occurrence from projections (under-counts projected expense)
- File: app/lib/logic/projectionEngine/loanProjections.ts : 31-43
- Why wrong: The amortization schedule always anchors its first date at rule.startDate (calculateAmortizationSchedule starts currentDate = new Date(config.startDate) and advances one month per step), and the schedule length is reduced to remainingPayments = termMonths - paymentsMade. It is NOT advanced past the already-paid months. When a loan payment is completed, markTransactionCompleteAction increments loanConfig.paymentsMade, so on regeneration the schedule produces remainingPayments steps but still starting at rule.startDate, i.e. the first regenerated step lands on the original (already-paid) start month. occurrenceId is derived from step.date, so the merger matches the earliest regenerated steps to the already-completed/stored occurrences and the schedule simply runs out before reaching the genuinely-remaining final month. Contrast with installmentProjections.ts (lines 35-37), which correctly advances currentDate past installmentsPaid months. Loans should do the same (start dates at the next unpaid payment, e.g. firstPaymentDate advanced by paymentsMade).
- Scenario: Monthly loan, startDate 2026-01-15, termMonths = 3. Initial projections: #1 2026-01-15 (occ rule_2026-01), #2 2026-02-15 (rule_2026-02), #3 2026-03-15 (rule_2026-03). User completes #1; paymentsMade becomes 1. Regeneration: remainingPayments = 2, schedule still starts at 2026-01-15 -> steps land on 2026-01-15 (rule_2026-01) and 2026-02-15 (rule_2026-02). The merger matches rule_2026-01 to the completed stored txn and shows rule_2026-02 as projected, but rule_2026-03 (the real outstanding final payment) is never generated. Projected expense for March is understated by one full payment and the calendar loses the payment. Correct: remaining schedule should cover Feb and March.
- Suggested fix: Advance the amortization schedule's start date by paymentsMade months (or use loanConfig.firstPaymentDate + paymentsMade) so regenerated steps align to the next UNPAID occurrence, matching the approach already used for installments.

### [F43] (critical/logic-error) Loan amortization schedule start date is not advanced by paymentsMade, so remaining payments are projected one month too early per completed payment
- File: app/lib/logic/projectionEngine/loanProjections.ts : 28-50
- Why wrong: When paymentsMade > 0, the code correctly shortens the term (remainingPayments) but regenerates the amortization schedule starting at parseDate(rule.startDate) — i.e. the ORIGINAL first payment date — instead of rule.startDate + paymentsMade months. calculateAmortizationSchedule emits its first step at startDate and then increments one month per step (currentDate.setMonth(getMonth()+1)). So the first *remaining* payment is placed at the loan's original first-payment month rather than the next unpaid month. Each completed payment shifts the entire remaining projection one month earlier. The correct behavior is to start the regenerated schedule at parseDate(rule.startDate) advanced by paymentsMade months (e.g. addMonths(parseDate(rule.startDate), paymentsMade)).
- Scenario: Loan firstPaymentDate (rule.startDate) = 2026-01-10, termMonths=12, paymentsMade=3 (Jan/Feb/Mar completed). remainingPayments=9. The code builds a 9-step schedule whose first step lands on 2026-01-10 (and subsequent steps Feb 10, Mar 10, Apr 10 ...). Correct: the next 9 payments should fall on 2026-04-10 .. 2026-12-10. Result: projected loan payments appear in already-paid months (Jan/Feb/Mar) and the projection ends in December instead of the true final month, mis-dating every remaining payment by 3 months. Downstream, dedup by month-based occurrenceId masks the duplicate Jan/Feb/Mar entries, but the genuinely-upcoming payments (which should be in later months) are silently dropped/shifted, so the day-balance calendar shows loan outflows in the wrong months.
- Suggested fix: Pass startDate: addMonths(parseDate(rule.startDate), loanConfig.paymentsMade) to calculateAmortizationSchedule so the regenerated remaining schedule begins on the next unpaid month.
- First-round verification: 3 votes, 0 refutes

### [F44] (high/bug) Loan paymentBreakdown.paymentNumber is computed from the post-filter array index, so it is wrong whenever the view window does not begin at the schedule's first payment
- File: app/lib/logic/projectionEngine/loanProjections.ts : 40-43
- Why wrong: `.filter()` runs before `.map()`, so `index` is the position within the windowed subset, not the absolute payment position. paymentNumber should reflect the payment's true position in the loan (1..termMonths). Because the index resets to 0 for the first in-window step, any projection window that starts after the loan's first payment month labels payments with numbers that are too low. (The credit-card builder avoids this by using the absolute step.month; the installment builder avoids it by indexing over the full remaining loop. Only the loan builder uses the post-filter index.)
- Scenario: Loan starts 2026-01-15, termMonths=24, paymentsMade=0. User views the calendar for June 2026 (viewStartDate=2026-06-01). The June payment is payment #6, but it is the first element of the filtered array (index=0), so paymentNumber = 0 + 0 + 1 = 1. The paymentBreakdown shows 'Payment 1 of 24' for the June payment and 'Payment 2 of 24' for July, etc. — every payment is mislabeled by the offset of the window. Correct paymentNumber for June is 6.
- Suggested fix: Compute the absolute index before filtering, e.g. schedule.map((step, absIndex) => ...).filter(...) or derive paymentNumber from the schedule position: const absoluteIndex = schedule.indexOf(step); paymentNumber = loanConfig.paymentsMade + absoluteIndex + 1. Simplest: map first (attaching paymentNumber = paymentsMade + index + 1 from the full schedule), then filter to the window.
- First-round verification: 3 votes, 0 refutes

### [F46] (high/correctness) Projected loan completion increments paymentsMade without reducing loanConfig.currentBalance, so the next projection regenerates an inflated EMI from the stale full balance
- File: app/contexts/FinancialContext/actions/transactionActions.ts : 123-131
- Why wrong: This is the completion path actually taken for projected loan transactions (id startsWith 'proj_'). It bumps paymentsMade but leaves loanConfig.currentBalance unchanged (the principal-reducing helper updateLoanBalance in firestore/expenseRules.ts is not called here). loanProjections.ts then regenerates the schedule with principal = loanConfig.currentBalance (still the original balance) and termMonths = remainingPayments (now smaller). Because calculateAmortizationSchedule recomputes the PMT from (full balance, fewer months), the projected monthly payment is LARGER than the true EMI, and the principal/interest breakdown no longer matches reality. The amount fed into createProjectedTransaction ({ ...rule, amount: step.payment }) is therefore overstated, inflating projected expenses and day balances.
- Scenario: Loan currentBalance = 12000, interestRate = 12% (1%/mo), termMonths = 12. True EMI ≈ 1066.19/mo. Complete one projected payment: paymentsMade becomes 1, currentBalance stays 12000, remainingPayments = 11. Next projection: calculateAmortizationSchedule(principal=12000, term=11) → PMT ≈ 1153.5/mo. Every remaining projected payment is shown as ~1153.5 instead of ~1066.19 (about +8% per payment), and the projected payoff total balloons well above the real loan cost.
- Suggested fix: On projected-loan completion, also reduce currentBalance by the principal portion of the completed payment (call updateLoanBalance, or replicate its newBalance = max(0, currentBalance - principalPaid) using the projection's paymentBreakdown.principalPaid). Keeping currentBalance consistent with paymentsMade makes the regenerated PMT equal the original EMI.
- First-round verification: 3 votes, 0 refutes
